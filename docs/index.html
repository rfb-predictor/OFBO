acceptor numberc back to ToC or Class ToC

IRI: https://w3id.org/ofbo#OFBO_0001013

The acceptor number quantifies the ability of a solvent to accept electron density from a Lewis base. It is determined relative to a reference scale in which hexane is assigned an acceptor number of zero. Higher AN values indicate stronger Lewis acidity of the solvent and greater ability to stabilize anions or electron-rich species. Typical examples include: hexane ≈ 0 acetonitrile ≈ 19 water ≈ 54
The acceptor number (AN) is an empirical measure of the Lewis acidity of a solvent. It is defined from the shift in the 31P NMR chemical shift of a standard probe molecule, typically triethylphosphine oxide (TEPO), when dissolved in the solvent.
